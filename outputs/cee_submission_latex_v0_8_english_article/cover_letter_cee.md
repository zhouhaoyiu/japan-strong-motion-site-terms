Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript entitled "Repeatable station terms redistribute long-period response spectra across Japanese strong-motion sites" for consideration as an Article in *Communications Earth & Environment*.

Regional ground-motion models leave station-associated residuals that can shift local response spectra. Their stability depends on the earthquake sample, ground-motion model and source-path distribution. This manuscript quantifies those dependencies and tests their effect on an official hazard-spectrum coordinate.

We separate event and station effects in 222,664 ground-surface K-NET and KiK-net records using MF2013-compatible RotD100 spectra at eight direct periods. A strict external test trains only on K-NET records from four earthquake groups and predicts KiK-net station terms estimated from the fifth group. At 3.0 s, the frozen model gives a correlation of 0.598 and a 20.8% root mean squared error reduction across 677 target stations. A chronological test then fits K-NET terms from the earliest 80% of dated events and predicts KiK-net terms from the latest 20%. It gives a correlation of 0.536 and a 19.4% error reduction across 547 target stations, with positive lower confidence bounds at all eight periods. The adverse reverse-direction result is retained as a sample-coverage boundary.

A national paired-sensor analysis adds 102,428 KiK-net surface--borehole spectra from 699 sites and 1,410 earthquakes. After removal of station-wide amplitude and the network-wide period mean, transfer and station-term spectral shapes correlate at 0.677. Their correlation remains 0.597--0.670, with a mean of 0.635, when the two spectra are estimated from disjoint earthquake groups. This frequency-dependent result connects the residual field to repeatable measured site response.

The manuscript links this validation to a public hazard product. Out-of-fold station predictions are propagated to matched J-SHIS response-spectrum ordinates after conversion from engineering bedrock to station surface conditions. At 3.0 s, the central 90% of primary station multipliers spans 0.624--1.338. Primary and frozen K-NET correction fields correlate at 0.736 and agree in direction at 79.7% of KiK-net sites. A calibrated 90% station-model interval excludes unity at 2.0% of sites, which states the point field's predictive limitation directly. A strict MF2013 regression-domain calculation reconstructs the Kanno-PGA cutoff and retains a station-field correlation of 0.959. The manuscript separates station-model prediction error from source, ground-motion-model and J-SHIS logic-tree uncertainty.

The analysis is relevant to *Communications Earth & Environment* because it combines national strong-motion coverage, reproducible validation and direct application to seismic-hazard representation. The matched-station calculation preserves the published J-SHIS source recurrence and logic tree. Its interpretation is limited to the average source-path distribution sampled by the strong-motion archive.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has been approved by all authors. Public data sources, redistribution limits, code, derived tables, funding, competing interests, author contributions and acknowledgements are stated in the manuscript. The submission includes a peer-review archive containing the analysis code and derived summary tables needed to verify the reported results. The same release will be made public on GitHub and archived with a DOI before publication.
