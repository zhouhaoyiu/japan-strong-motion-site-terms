Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript entitled "Repeatable station terms redistribute long-period response spectra across Japanese strong-motion sites" for consideration as an Article in *Communications Earth & Environment*.

Regional ground-motion models leave station-associated residuals that can shift local response spectra. Their transfer depends on the earthquake sample, ground-motion backbone and source-path distribution. This manuscript quantifies those dependencies and tests their effect on an official hazard-spectrum coordinate.

We separate event and station effects in 222,664 ground-surface K-NET and KiK-net records using MF2013-compatible RotD100 spectra at eight direct periods. The station field is tested with disjoint earthquake groups, a Zhao 2006 calculation, spatial blocks, cross-network transfer, fixed macroregions, influential-event deletion and path/source stratification. At 3.0 s, event-group station fields correlate at 0.921, while directional-sector correlations with the full field range from 0.280 to 0.886. This contrast identifies a repeatable station-associated average and a distinct path-conditioned component.

The distinct contribution is a traceable transfer audit linked to a public hazard product. Out-of-fold station predictions are propagated to matched J-SHIS response-spectrum ordinates after conversion from engineering bedrock to station surface conditions. At 3.0 s, the central 90% of station multipliers spans 0.624--1.338 and changes the matched-station median surface ordinate from 0.081 to 0.075 g. The manuscript retains adverse spatial-fold results and separates station-model prediction error from source, backbone and J-SHIS logic-tree uncertainty.

The work fits *Communications Earth & Environment* through its national strong-motion coverage, reproducible model auditing and direct relevance to seismic-hazard representation. The matched-station calculation preserves the published J-SHIS source recurrence and logic tree. Its interpretation is explicitly limited to the average source-path distribution sampled by the strong-motion archive.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has been approved by all authors. Public data sources, redistribution limits, code, derived tables, funding, competing interests, author contributions and acknowledgements are stated in the manuscript.
