Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript entitled "Long-period site residuals and response-spectrum hazard sensitivity in Japanese strong-motion records" for consideration as an Article in Communications Earth & Environment.

Official long-period response-spectrum maps are widely used for site screening and engineering comparisons, but regional ground-motion models average repeated station response. This manuscript shows that Japanese strong-motion records contain a stable 1--3 s non-ergodic station term that is predictable from public site variables and large enough to change official J-SHIS response-spectrum ordinates.

We analyze 333,808 K-NET and KiK-net records from the public J-SHIS/NIED flatfile with the Morikawa and Fujiwara Japan ground-motion model. MF2013 D1400/AVS30 site terms reduce mean absolute residuals by 16.1% at SA(1.0 s) and 21.2% at SA(3.0 s), compared with 1.6% for PGA and 3.9% at SA(0.3 s). The SA(3.0 s) station-residual correlation with log10(D1400) decreases from 0.614 to -0.025 after site correction.

The residual signal is stable and hazard-facing. Spatial-block held-out station models reduce RMSE by 38.9% at SA(1.0 s) and 44.3% at SA(3.0 s). An interpretable site-variable model using basin depth, shallow velocity, borehole, and location variables recovers part of the SA(3.0 s) term without station identifiers. Applying station multipliers to official J-SHIS response-spectrum meshes changes the 50-year 10% median SA(3.0 s) ordinate from 0.076 to 0.048 g. All-period propagation on 10,467 sampled meshes gives median multipliers of 1.045 at 0.3 s, 0.834 at 1.0 s, and 0.658 at 3.0--5.0 s.

The manuscript keeps the official hazard boundary explicit. Public J-SHIS source-parameter checks, hazard-curve API comparisons, GSI terrain, VS400 site-amplification fields, borehole profiles, and multi-event KiK-net transfer functions support traceability and physical interpretation. Source recurrence, spatial correlation, model uncertainty, and logic-tree aggregation remain in the official J-SHIS product.

This work fits Communications Earth & Environment because it links dense public earthquake observations to a reproducible response-spectrum sensitivity test for seismic-hazard assessment. It provides an observation-to-spectrum workflow for identifying long-period non-ergodic site response in a national hazard-map setting.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has been approved by all authors.
