Dear Editors,

We submit "Repeatable station terms modify fixed-probability long-period response spectra at stations across Japan" for consideration as an Article in *Communications Earth & Environment*.

Regional ground-motion models describe average shaking, while repeatable station-specific residuals can change local response spectra. We analyse 222,664 K-NET and KiK-net surface records at eight directly reported RotD100 periods. The study tests the repeatability, physical interpretation and spatial estimation of station terms, then propagates cross-validated estimates to fixed-probability J-SHIS response spectra.

At 3.0 s, station terms estimated from independent earthquake groups correlate at 0.890. A relation calibrated with K-NET records estimates KiK-net terms from disjoint earthquakes with a correlation of 0.598 and 20.8% lower root mean squared error. In the chronological test, the earliest 80% of dated K-NET earthquakes estimate KiK-net terms from the latest 20% with a correlation of 0.536 and 19.4% lower error; all eight period-specific lower confidence bounds remain positive. Across 102,428 paired KiK-net surface-to-borehole spectra, response-spectrum-ratio and station-term shapes calculated from disjoint earthquake groups correlate at 0.635.

Two external comparisons define the physical and geographic limits. At 5.0 s, record-derived crustal station terms correlate at 0.304 with an independent Kanto simulation across 364 stations; the 95% interval is 0.163--0.434 after resampling 31.7 km spatial blocks. Cross-validated crustal estimates correlate at 0.135 with the simulation. Source conditioning separates the long-period result: intraplate terms reduce spatial error by 14.3--14.8% at 3.0--5.0 s, while several crustal and interplate results are weak or adverse. In 13,430 post-2015 European-Mediterranean records, independent-earthquake-group terms correlate at 0.928 at 3.0 s. The fixed Japanese relation gives a correlation of 0.047 in Europe, and locally fitted ESM relations increase spatial-block error by 3.6--7.6%.

We propagate only cross-validated station terms to matched J-SHIS spectral accelerations after converting the engineering-bedrock reference to each station's surface condition. The central 90% of 3.0 s multipliers spans 0.624--1.338. Independent Japanese estimates agree in direction at 79.7% of KiK-net sites, while a calibrated 90% station-model interval excludes unity at 2.0%. J-SHIS subduction spectral acceleration is at least as large as the active-shallow value at 1,428 of 1,628 stations. Separate crustal, interplate and intraplate terms provide a source-conditioned sensitivity. This calculation evaluates published fixed-probability map values; a source-level PSHA recomputation requires source recurrence and weights that these products do not provide. A strict MF2013 regression-domain analysis, including the Kanno-PGA cutoff, retains a 3.0 s station-pattern correlation of 0.959 with the primary result.

The manuscript links national strong-motion observations with paired surface and borehole sensors, an independent 3D simulation, European-Mediterranean records and public hazard products. The analysis reports the adverse cross-region and source-conditioned results and separates station-term repeatability, spatial-estimation error and hazard-model uncertainty.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere and has been approved by both authors. Public data sources, redistribution limits, code, derived tables, funding, competing interests, author contributions and acknowledgements are stated in the manuscript.
